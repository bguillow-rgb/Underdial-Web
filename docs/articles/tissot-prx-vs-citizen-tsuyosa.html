Tissot PRX vs Citizen Tsuyosa: Which Should You Buy?

By Underdial · Published September 11, 2026

Quick Answer

Both are excellent integrated-bracelet watches under $500, but they aim at different buyers. The Tissot PRX wins on Swiss heritage, slimmer profile, and finishing. The Citizen Tsuyosa wins on value-per-spec, bolder dial colors, and sapphire crystal across more references. Choose PRX for understated refinement; choose Tsuyosa for personality and price.

The integrated-bracelet sports watch is the defining value-category story of the last few years, and two names come up in nearly every forum thread and comparison video: the Tissot PRX and the Citizen Tsuyosa. Both hover near the $400-$500 mark, both flow from case to bracelet without a visible seam, and both punch well above their price in visual impact. But they are genuinely different watches, built around different philosophies, and picking the wrong one is a real risk. Here’s what actually separates them.

What exactly am I choosing between with these two watches?

A question we hear often: buyers assume the PRX and Tsuyosa are basically the same watch at slightly different prices. They’re not.

The PRX is a Swiss-made watch drawing on 1970s integrated-bracelet design language: sharp angles, a tapered bracelet, and a slim, dressy profile. The Tsuyosa goes in a different direction. Its fixed aluminum bezel with engraved markings and oversized crown give it the dimensional feel of a tool watch. Dial colors run from quiet black and grey all the way to gold and blue, which tells you something about the intended mood.

In short: the PRX is refined and lean; the Tsuyosa is bolder and louder. Neither is wrong, but they suit different people.

How do the movements actually compare?

This is where the specs look deceptively lopsided, and where you need to read carefully.

The PRX Powermatic 80 runs an ETA-based caliber (the ETA C07.111, derived from the 2824). That movement is known for its reliability and an 80-hour power reserve, nearly double the Tsuyosa’s. The finishing is also noticeably better, consistent with what you’d expect from a Swiss manufacture at this price.

The Tsuyosa uses Citizen’s in-house caliber 8210, beating at 21,600 VPH with a 40-hour power reserve. It’s a reliable, proven movement. Not a showpiece, but not a liability either.

So the PRX wins on power reserve and movement finishing. The Tsuyosa hits back with something worth taking seriously: Citizen also makes an Eco-Drive version, which runs indefinitely on ambient light and never needs winding or a battery. For someone who rotates several watches and forgets to wind things, that’s a meaningful ownership difference.

If you’re coming from our automatic vs quartz guide, this is exactly the trade-off laid out there: mechanical prestige versus practical convenience.

Which watch has better finishing and bracelet quality?

This one comes up a lot. Side-by-side photos flatter both watches, but in-hand the differences become clear.

The PRX’s butterfly clasp is milled; the Tsuyosa’s is stamped steel. Functional, yes, but the gap is noticeable. The PRX also carries a signed crown, a small detail that adds to the overall feel of the thing.

Both bracelets articulate well and taper toward the clasp for comfort. The PRX feels slightly more architectural; the Tsuyosa’s is a touch softer in wear.

The Tsuyosa does hold its own in one area: its exhibition caseback lets you watch the movement, which adds appeal at the lower price point. The PRX Powermatic 80 offers the same, but it’s more expected at that tier.

Net result: the PRX edges ahead on bracelet quality and clasp execution. The Tsuyosa compensates with a livelier movement display and beefier case dimensions if you prefer something with more physical presence.

Which one actually fits my wrist better?

Wrist fit is one of the most under-discussed variables in an integrated-bracelet comparison, because the integrated design makes case size and bracelet taper work together in ways a regular lugged watch doesn’t.

The PRX’s automatic version runs 40mm, and the case is slim enough to slip under a shirt cuff with minimal drama. Quartz references start at 35mm, which is genuinely versatile. The cushion shape and 1970s proportions mean it reads smaller than the number suggests.

The Tsuyosa is 42mm across most references, which wears as a confidently sized watch. On wrists under about 17cm, that 42mm with a wide integrated bracelet can feel dominant. On larger wrists (18cm or more), it sits beautifully.

Sub-$500 is the range where you stop seeing fashion watches with plastic cases and start seeing sapphire crystals, automatic movements, and brand heritage that actual collectors take seriously. Both of these watches clear that bar.

What do the dial options look like, and which ages better?

Readers frequently ask: will I get bored of this watch in two years?

The PRX’s palette trends conservative: navy, silver, white, black, with classic waffle textures on automatics. That’s a feature, not a flaw, if you want a watch that works across every context.

The Tsuyosa brings an energetic color range: greens, blues, yellows, sunbursts. Genuinely fun, and they stand out on a wrist in a way the PRX doesn’t try to. Buyers who prefer a watch that disappears into any outfit will find the PRX’s restraint more enduring. Buyers who want their watch to be the first thing someone notices will gravitate toward the Tsuyosa.

Both have held up well since their respective launches. The PRX’s 1970s design language has been in fashion for several years and shows no sign of dating. The Tsuyosa’s tool-watch proportions are similarly durable. Neither is a trend watch, which matters when you’re spending $400 or more.

So which one should I actually buy?

The PRX delivers sharp, retro-Swiss geometry with a refined dress-sport personality. The Tsuyosa reads more playful and modern, with softer curves and colors the PRX wouldn’t dare attempt. On the wrist, both feel more expensive than they are. The call is aesthetic: crisp heritage (PRX) versus colorful contemporary (Tsuyosa).

Use this as your decision filter:

Buy the Tissot PRX if:

  • You want Swiss Made provenance and a movement with an 80-hour reserve
  • You have a smaller wrist (under 17.5cm) or prefer slim under-cuff wear
  • Your wardrobe skews formal or business-casual and you want one watch that disappears into every outfit
  • You appreciate better clasp and bracelet execution at the cost of a slightly higher price

Buy the Citizen Tsuyosa if:

  • You want the best headline specs (sapphire plus automatic) at the lowest price
  • Your wrist is 17.5cm or larger and a 42mm case suits you
  • You want expressive dial color options: teal, sunburst blue, vivid yellow
  • You’re rotating a collection and want zero-maintenance via Eco-Drive

If you already own a dress watch or a field watch and want an integrated sports piece that doubles as a weekend statement, the Tsuyosa is the faster route to something visually distinct. If you want something that quietly elevates a business look without drawing attention to itself, the PRX is the smarter fit. Either way, run both through Underdial against your existing collection before committing. Buying a third blue-dial watch when you already own two is a mistake both of these watches make embarrassingly easy.

FAQs

Is the Citizen Tsuyosa cheaper than the Tissot PRX? Generally yes. The Tsuyosa automatic typically retails $50-$100 below the PRX Powermatic 80, and Eco-Drive versions can be even more affordable. Street pricing shifts, so check current prices before buying.

Which has a better movement — the Tissot PRX or Citizen Tsuyosa? Depends on your priorities. The PRX Powermatic 80 (an ETA-based caliber) offers an exceptional 80-hour power reserve and superior finishing. The Tsuyosa runs Citizen’s in-house caliber 8210 with roughly 40-45 hours of reserve: reliable and proven, but less impressive on paper.

Does the Citizen Tsuyosa have a sapphire crystal? Yes. The Tsuyosa automatic references ship with a sapphire crystal, which is a notable win at its price point and matches what the PRX offers.

Which watch wears better on a smaller wrist? The Tissot PRX has an edge here. Its cushion-shaped case starts at 35mm (quartz) and runs 40mm (automatic), with a slimmer profile. The Tsuyosa is a uniform 42mm across most references, which can read large on wrists under 17cm.
