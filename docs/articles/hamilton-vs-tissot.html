Hamilton vs Tissot: Which Swiss Watch Should You Buy?

By Underdial · Published September 17, 2026

Quick Answer

Hamilton and Tissot share the same Swatch Group movements and Swiss-made credentials, but they pull in opposite directions: Hamilton leans into military heritage and automatic-first character, while Tissot offers broader style range and lower entry prices. Buy Hamilton for personality; buy Tissot for versatility and value.

Both Hamilton and Tissot are Swiss-made, both live inside the Swatch Group, and — here’s the part most buyers don’t realise until they dig into the specs — both brands share identical ETA and Powermatic 80 movements while projecting completely different personalities. That makes the choice less about movement quality and almost entirely about identity, design, and which price tier actually fits your life. This guide cuts through the overlap.

So what actually separates Hamilton and Tissot if the movements are the same?

The short answer: everything except the engine.

Hamilton brings American heritage, military history, and Hollywood glamour to the Swiss watchmaking formula. Tissot offers the most globally recognised entry into Swiss horology, powered by massive sports sponsorships and design hits like the viral PRX.

In practice, that plays out in a few concrete ways. Design language: Hamilton’s catalogue is dominated by field watches, pilot references, and vintage-military silhouettes — purposeful, sometimes austere, almost always masculine in proportion. Tissot’s range is far wider, spanning integrated sports bracelets (PRX), classic dress (Le Locle), chronographs, and solar-quartz outdoors pieces. Entry price: Tissot’s quartz options start well below $300, whereas Hamilton’s cheapest automatics sit in the $495—$595 range. Cultural weight: Hamilton appeals to buyers who want bold designs and military roots; Tissot is known for clean styling, wide model variety, and genuinely accessible price points.

Neither is objectively superior. They are different watches for different buyers.

Wait — they’re both Swatch Group brands. Does that mean they’re basically the same watch?

A question we hear often.

Not quite. Hamilton and Tissot share identical movements from the Swatch Group’s ETA supply chain. The Powermatic 80, with its 80-hour power reserve, is the primary automatic calibre for both brands, and standard ETA quartz covers the lower-priced models. So yes, the movement inside a Hamilton Khaki Mechanical and a Tissot Gentleman Powermatic 80 are cousins from the same factory.

Being siblings inside a conglomerate doesn’t make watches identical, though — any more than the VW Golf and Audi A3 sharing a platform makes them the same car. Case finishing, dial execution, bracelet quality, and brand storytelling all differ, and at $500—$800 those differences are real and visible. Both brands have access to COSC chronometer certification and Nivachron anti-magnetic hairsprings on select references, so even at the top of the spec sheet they’re working with the same toolkit.

What you’re really paying for is the story on the dial and the design sensibility around it.

Which brand has better heritage — Hamilton or Tissot?

Hamilton’s narrative is one of the richest in affordable watchmaking. Founded in Lancaster, Pennsylvania in 1892, the brand supplied watches to the US military in both World Wars and became the official timekeeper of American railroads. It has appeared in more than 500 films — think Interstellar, Men in Black, Elvis — a track record that gives even its most basic field watch a certain cinematic gravity.

Tissot has been around since 1853, making it one of the oldest continuously operating watch companies in the world. Its heritage is less Hollywood drama and more Swiss consistency: precision timing for MotoGP, NBA, and cycling, plus a long record of watchmaking firsts including the first plastic movement and the first tactile touch screen in a watch.

Hamilton wins on brand narrative, combining railroads, military service, and Hollywood into a genuinely compelling story, though Tissot’s global sports presence is formidable. If heritage resonates with you — and for many watch buyers it genuinely does — Hamilton’s story is hard to beat under $1,000.

I’m buying my first Swiss watch. Which should I start with?

This one comes up a lot.

First-time Swiss buyers tend to land in one of two camps, and each brand serves a different one clearly.

If you want maximum versatility — a watch that works for a first date, a job interview, and a Saturday morning errand run — the Tissot PRX Powermatic 80 or the Tissot Gentleman gives you a cleaner, more neutral starting point. Tissot’s range spans dress watches, sport chronographs, and dive pieces, and the aesthetic is generally more contemporary. That’s worth something when you’re still working out what kind of watch person you are.

If you already know you gravitate toward military aesthetics, tool watches, or the idea of a watch with a genuine story behind it, the Hamilton Khaki Field Mechanical at $495 is one of the most honest watches available at the price. Hand-wound movement, canvas strap, legible field-watch dial — everything feels considered rather than compromised. The risk with the Khaki Field as a first watch is that its look is committed: it won’t dress up for a formal dinner. The PRX can.

Does one brand hold its value better than the other?

Readers frequently ask.

Honestly, neither holds value impressively in the sub-$1,000 segment — and that’s not a knock on either brand, it’s just the reality of the price tier. Secondary market data tracked across major platforms shows most pre-owned Hamilton and Tissot watches selling for roughly 40—60% of retail within two years of purchase. A new Tissot PRX costs $695 retail; the same watch in excellent condition trades for around $650 pre-owned. The savings are modest, but you avoid the initial depreciation entirely.

The Hamilton Khaki Field has a small secondary-market advantage driven by collector interest and the cult around the hand-wound Mechanical variant. Discontinued references sometimes command above-retail prices, but that’s the exception, not a reason to buy speculatively. If resale matters deeply to your decision, the do watches hold their value article covers the full picture for this price tier.

Buy both brands to wear, not to flip.

Which brand is easier to service long-term?

Both benefit from Swatch Group’s massive global service network. Hamilton and Tissot are the two most accessible Swiss-made brands in the Swatch Group family, so authorised service centres exist in most major cities worldwide. Movement parts — particularly the Powermatic 80 and ETA 2824-2 — are among the most widely available calibre components in the industry, which keeps third-party service costs competitive.

A well-regulated Sellita (or ETA equivalent) is more reliable and far cheaper to service than a poorly executed in-house calibre, and parts availability over a thirty-year horizon favours the common movement. That’s a genuine long-term advantage for both brands over many microbrands and niche independents. Expect a full service to cost $150—$250 at an independent watchmaker, or closer to $300—$400 through an authorised Swatch Group dealer.

FAQs

Are Hamilton and Tissot the same quality? Yes, broadly. Both are Swiss-made Swatch Group brands using the same ETA and Powermatic 80 movements, with similar sapphire crystals and case finishing at comparable price points. Neither holds a meaningful quality edge over the other — the differences are design philosophy and brand identity.

Which is more expensive, Hamilton or Tissot? Hamilton skews slightly higher on average. Its automatic lineup starts around $495—$595, while Tissot’s entry-level automatics start around $575, but Tissot’s quartz range dips below $300 — lower than most Hamilton equivalents.

Do Hamilton watches hold their value better than Tissot? Neither brand retains value strongly under $1,000 — most pre-owned examples sell for 40—60% of retail. The Hamilton Khaki Field has a slight edge in secondary-market demand due to its cult following, but neither approaches the resale stability of Rolex or even Omega.

Is Hamilton really Swiss-made even though it has an American name? Yes. Hamilton was founded in Lancaster, Pennsylvania in 1892 but moved all manufacturing to Biel, Switzerland in 1969. Every Hamilton watch today carries a genuine ‘Swiss Made’ designation and shares facilities with Swatch Group stablemates like ETA.

Which watch is better for everyday wear — Hamilton or Tissot? Both are excellent daily wearers, but the answer depends on your job and style. The Tissot PRX’s integrated bracelet and slim profile suit office or business-casual environments. The Hamilton Khaki Field’s canvas-strap ruggedness and legible dial handle everything from a desk to a hike without looking out of place.
