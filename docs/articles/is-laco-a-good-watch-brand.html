Is Laco a Good Watch Brand? An Honest Answer

By Underdial · Published November 14, 2026

Quick Answer

Yes, Laco is a legitimate German watch brand founded in 1925 and one of the original makers of WWII-era Flieger observation watches. It's a good buy if you want that specific pilot-watch design language and heritage story. It's not the right fit if you want a versatile everyday watch.

Laco sits in an interesting corner of the under-$1,000 market: genuinely historic, narrowly focused, and completely unknown to most watch buyers until they go looking for a Flieger. If you’ve already looked at the Hamilton Khaki Pilot or browsed pilot watch vs field watch comparisons and kept arriving back at the same German aesthetic, Laco is probably what you were circling around without knowing the name. This guide answers whether the brand deserves your money, and whether you’re the right buyer for it.

Is Laco actually a historic brand, or is the heritage marketing?

The heritage is real. Laco, originally Lacher & Co, was founded in Pforzheim, Germany in 1925, making it a century-old watch manufacturer by any honest count. More specifically, Laco Uhrenmanufaktur was founded during the 1920s, when Swiss movements were widely used across the Pforzheim watch industry. What gives the brand its strongest claim to authenticity is its role in the Second World War: Laco was among the original makers contracted to produce the German military B-Uhr, the Beobachtungsuhr, or observer watch, the large-cased, highly legible instrument worn by Luftwaffe navigators. That direct lineage is not a story invented by a marketing team decades later.

This matters because the word “Flieger” gets used loosely across the watch industry. Dozens of brands sell pilot-aesthetic watches with no historical connection to actual aviation timekeeping. Laco is one of a handful of brands, alongside Stowa and IWC, that can trace a genuine B-Uhr provenance. If that specificity matters to you, the brand earns its position. If you just like the look, there are cheaper options that deliver a similar silhouette.

What do you actually get for $400-$800?

A question we hear often: what does a Laco watch deliver in real-world terms, beyond the history?

Most Laco pilot references land between roughly $400 and $800 new, putting them in direct competition with the Hamilton Khaki Pilot, mid-spec Tissot models, and the lower end of the Oris catalogue. At that price, here’s the honest breakdown:

FeatureWhat Laco delivers
MovementETA or Sellita Swiss automatic (not in-house)
Case finishingSolid, brushed steel — functional rather than decorative
Dial qualityStrong — legible, historically accurate Type A or Type B layouts
CrystalMineral on most references; sapphire on select higher-tier models
Case sizeTypically 36mm-42mm, with some larger vintage-inspired references
HeritageGenuine B-Uhr lineage from 1940s production contracts
VersatilityLow — optimised for pilot-watch styling

The case quality is solid without being exceptional, comparable to German-designed brands at similar prices. You are not buying Laco for extraordinary case finishing or exhibition casebacks. You are buying it because the dial is exactly right, and no Swiss watch at this price will get the B-Uhr typography and crown design as close to the original as Laco does.

The movement picture is worth understanding clearly. Most Laco automatics run Swiss ETA or Sellita calibres. These are reliable, serviceable movements used throughout the sub-$1,000 Swiss watch world, but they are not a unique Laco selling point. The heritage story lives in the case and dial, not the calibre.

How does Laco compare to Hamilton for a pilot watch?

This one comes up a lot: Laco vs Hamilton is the comparison most buyers land on after their first research session, because both brands occupy similar price territory and both claim pilot-watch credentials.

The honest answer is that they are built for different things. Hamilton’s Khaki Pilot is a more flexible piece. It sits comfortably under a shirt cuff, translates from casual to business-casual without effort, and is widely available at authorised dealers across the US. Laco’s most recognisable designs are less accommodating. If you want a watch that disappears under a cuff, Laco’s signature look may work against you.

If the Flieger aesthetic is a secondary consideration and you want a pilot-inspired watch you can wear everywhere, Hamilton wins. If the B-Uhr aesthetic is the entire point, large onion crown, clean observer-dial numerals, unambiguous aviation identity, Laco is the more honest expression of that idea. Which Laco model should I actually start with?

Readers frequently ask: which reference is the right entry point, given how many Laco models exist.

The Laco Augsburg is the standard answer, and it earns that recommendation. It’s the reference that most closely follows the original Type A B-Uhr layout: Arabic numerals at 3, 6, 9, and 12, a clean sub-seconds dial, and the characteristic oversized crown, at the lower end of Laco’s price range, typically $400-$600 new. At that price, an automatic pilot watch from a German brand with Laco’s history is easier to justify than a higher-priced watch if you’re still deciding whether the style fits your life.

The Friedrichshafen is worth considering if you want something slightly more restrained. It’s narrower and reads a little dressier without abandoning the pilot-watch identity. For buyers who specifically want the Type B dial (triangle at 12 o’clock instead of numerals), the Laco Pilot Original series offers that variant. Avoid buying on case diameter alone: Laco produces some very large references that read as costume pieces on a standard wrist. Check lug-to-lug against your wrist before committing. Who should buy a Laco, and who should skip it?

Buy a Laco if you want a pilot watch for the design story, not just the silhouette. Specifically: if you’ve looked at the Hamilton Khaki Pilot and found it too generic, if you’ve already owned a field watch and want something more singular, or if the B-Uhr lineage genuinely interests you, Laco delivers something no Swiss brand at this price replicates.

Skip Laco if you want a small dress watch, a flashy luxury logo, or a watch that can pass as anything other than what it is. The brand’s design identity is narrow by design. That’s a feature for the right buyer and a real limitation for everyone else. It’s also worth noting that Laco has limited authorised dealer presence in North America compared to Hamilton or Tissot, so after-sales service and strap availability require a bit more planning.

This is not a universally recommended brand the way Seiko or Tissot is. It’s a correct answer to a specific question.

Is Laco good value compared to other German watch brands?

German-designed watch brands under $1,000 form a loose cluster that includes Laco, Zeppelin, Sternglass, and Stowa, each positioned slightly differently. Zeppelin draws on airship iconography and a broader catalogue. Laco stays closer to the pure Flieger brief. Both offer genuine German aviation heritage design and ETA-based automatic quality that no Swiss competitor at the same price quite replicates.

Sternglass offers cleaner Bauhaus-influenced dials at a lower price point, with Swiss quartz or Miyota automatics rather than ETA. If the B-Uhr dial is the draw, Laco is the more historically grounded choice. If you just want a good-looking minimalist German watch, Sternglass gets you there for less money. Start with the design you actually want, then find the brand that gets you there most honestly.

According to watch industry data, the under-$800 Flieger segment is growing, driven partly by buyers who’ve exhausted the mainstream Seiko and Tissot recommendations and are looking for something with a narrower, more defined identity. Laco sits in a genuinely interesting position in that market: too specific to recommend broadly, but arguably the most authentic choice for the buyer it’s designed for.

FAQs

Is Laco a reputable watch brand? Yes. Laco was founded in Pforzheim, Germany in 1925 and was one of the original manufacturers contracted to produce the German military B-Uhr observer watch during WWII. That genuine heritage makes it one of the most credible affordable Flieger brands available today.

Where are Laco watches made? Laco watches are designed and assembled in Pforzheim, Germany. Most models use Swiss automatic movements, primarily ETA or Sellita sourced calibres, rather than in-house German movements.

How much do Laco watches cost? Most Laco pilot watches sit between roughly $400 and $800 new. Entry automatic Flieger references like the Augsburg start around $400-$600, while more complex or larger models approach $800. That positions them directly against Hamilton and mid-tier Tissot.

What is the best Laco watch to buy? The Laco Augsburg is the most recommended starting point: it closely follows the original Type A B-Uhr dial layout, uses a reliable automatic movement, and comes in at the lower end of the brand’s price range. If you want something smaller and dressier, the Friedrichshafen is worth considering.

How does Laco compare to Hamilton in pilot watches? Hamilton’s Khaki Pilot is Swiss-made, slightly more versatile (it reads as a field watch as much as a pilot watch), and is widely available at similar prices. Laco goes deeper on authentic B-Uhr design fidelity: larger onion crowns, cleaner observer-watch dials. It’s the better pick if the Flieger aesthetic is the whole point rather than one option among many.
