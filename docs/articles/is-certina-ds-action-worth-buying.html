Is the Certina DS Action Worth Buying? An Honest Answer

By Underdial · Published November 22, 2026

Quick Answer

Yes, the Certina DS Action is worth buying for most people who want a legitimate Swiss automatic dive watch under $1,000. You get ISO 6425 certification, an ETA Powermatic 80 with 80-hour power reserve, sapphire crystal, and a ceramic bezel at a price that significantly undercuts comparable Swiss competitors.

The Certina DS Action Diver has earned a quiet reputation in watch circles as one of the best-kept secrets in Swiss watchmaking: a full-spec dive watch from a 130-year-old Swatch Group brand that most people outside the hobby have never heard of. If you’re weighing it against the Tissot Seastar, the Seiko Prospex, or just trying to work out whether the brand is worth trusting at all, here’s the clearest answer we can give you.

So, is the Certina DS Action actually worth the money?

The short answer is yes, and unusually decisively for a watch in this price range.

The DS Action Diver delivers a 300-metre ISO 6425-certified rating, a ceramic bezel insert, sapphire crystal, screw-down crown, and a solid caseback, all combined with the ETA Powermatic 80 movement, for under $1,000. That is a spec sheet many watches at twice the price struggle to match in full.

Certina flies under the radar because it doesn’t invest in celebrity endorsements, but informed buyers recognise the value: Swiss-made, ETA-powered, and built to take a beating. That low profile actually works in your favour as a buyer. You’re paying for the watch, not the marketing.

What exactly do you get for the price?

A question we hear often: what does the DS Action’s spec sheet look like, and does the real-world experience match what’s written down?

The DS Action Diver comes in 38mm, 40.5mm, and 43mm case sizes. The 38mm version specs out at $835, with a 44.9mm lug-to-lug, 12.2mm thickness, 19mm lug width, 200m water resistance, and an automatic Powermatic 80.611 movement. If you want the titanium version, Certina offers it at an even $1,000.

The centrepiece is the movement. The DS Action uses the Swatch Group’s Powermatic 80 caliber, whose headline feature is an 80-hour power reserve. To get there, it runs at a slower 21,600vph, but it also offers hacking seconds and a quick-set date. The Nivachron hairspring, a titanium alloy, adds real resistance to magnetism and temperature swings. That’s something you don’t see discussed on most competitors’ spec pages.

In real-world testing, the DS Action has been found running within a few seconds per day, which is perfectly fine at this price. One independent reviewer using a timegrapher reported even better: the DS Action GMT ran at 0 to +1 seconds per day, among the best results they had tested.

Why haven’t I heard of Certina before?

This one comes up a lot: Certina is a Swiss brand founded in 1888 that sits inside the Swatch Group alongside Tissot, Longines, and Omega. It has long been associated with reliable sports watches, but unlike Tissot, it has never had a major marketing push or a hero model that broke into the mainstream conversation.

That’s genuinely unusual at this tier. Most affordable Swiss watches achieve recognition through one flagship model: Tissot’s PRX, Hamilton’s Khaki Field, Longines’ HydroConquest. Certina has the DS Action Diver, which is arguably more capable than any of them on a pure dive-watch spec comparison, yet it barely registers in mainstream watch media.

The result is that pricing stays honest. Looking at comparable watches, the DS Action offers great value for its asking price, either better than or mostly equal to the specs of competitors, and by far the least expensive. If you want the most capable watch per dollar and don’t need a recognisable logo on your wrist, Certina rewards exactly that mindset.

Is the ISO 6425 certification actually important?

Readers frequently ask: does it matter whether a dive watch has ISO 6425 or just a 300m rating?

It matters more than the number alone. A watch labelled “300m water resistant” has passed a static pressure test in a lab. ISO 6425 goes further: it mandates minimum luminescence performance, bezel resistance, legibility, shock resistance, and magnetic resistance testing on top of water resistance. For serious divers, the DS Action Diver’s ISO 6425 certification is a meaningful distinction when making a purchase decision.

For recreational swimmers and snorkellers, the practical difference is small. For anyone who actually dives, or who simply wants confirmation that their watch has been tested to a professional standard, it’s not a minor footnote. The Certina carrying this certification at under $1,000 puts it in the same category as dive watches costing two or three times more. Very few Swiss watches at this price can say the same.

What are the legitimate criticisms of the DS Action?

Fairness requires this section. The DS Action Diver is not perfect at any size.

The 40.5mm version has a lug-to-lug of 48mm and a case thickness of 14.1mm, which makes it chunkier than some competitive references from Seiko and relative newcomers like Serica and Baltic. If you have a smaller wrist or prefer a low-profile daily watch, that thickness is noticeable.

The movement, while reliable, is not chronometer-certified. If that specification matters to you in a Swiss automatic under $1,000, you’d need to look at Certina’s Chronometer variants or step up to a different tier entirely.

The bracelet on the steel models is functional rather than refined. Reviewers consistently describe it as secure and practical, but short on the brushed-and-polished finishing contrast you’d find on a Tissot or Hamilton at this price. It’s a tool-watch bracelet on a tool watch, which is either a feature or a flaw depending on what you’re after.

Finally, most watches under $1,000 will lose 20-40% of their value after purchase. The DS Action is no exception. Buy it to wear, not as a hedge.

Should I buy the 38mm, 40.5mm, or 43mm?

A question we hear often: which DS Action size is actually the right one?

The 38mm is the choice for anyone with a wrist under about 17cm, or anyone who finds most dive watches unwearable off the beach. The word “entry-level” only applies to its positioning within the range, not to its specification: 300-metre water resistance and the same Powermatic 80 movement. The 44.9mm lug-to-lug keeps it genuinely wearable.

The 40.5mm is the most versatile all-rounder and the size most reviewers recommend first. It wears closer to a 41mm on the wrist, the bracelet options are more varied, and its footprint matches the Rolex Submariner.

The 43mm is for buyers who want maximum visual presence on larger wrists. Its lug-to-lug stretches to 51.44mm, which will overhang a 17cm wrist. Worth checking against your actual wrist measurement before committing, which is exactly what Underdial’s wrist-fit check is built for.

Five FAQs on the Certina DS Action

What movement does the Certina DS Action use? The DS Action Diver runs the ETA Powermatic 80.611 automatic, made within the Swatch Group. It delivers an 80-hour power reserve, hacking seconds, and a Nivachron hairspring that resists magnetism. It is not in-house, but it is proven, serviceable, and among the most dependable movements in affordable Swiss watchmaking.

Is the Certina DS Action ISO 6425 certified? Yes. The DS Action Diver models carry ISO 6425 certification, which means water resistance was verified against a strict international standard rather than just a lab rating. That matters for anyone who plans to actually dive with the watch.

How does the Certina DS Action compare to the Tissot Seastar? Both use the ETA Powermatic 80 and offer strong Swiss dive credentials. The DS Action adds ISO 6425 certification and Certina’s shock resistance system; the Seastar is thinner and slightly more relaxed for everyday office wear. For committed divers, the DS Action wins on spec. For all-around versatility, the Seastar is easier to dress up.

What sizes does the Certina DS Action Diver come in? Certina currently offers the DS Action Diver in 38mm, 40.5mm, and 43mm, all in stainless steel, with titanium variants available on select references. The 38mm suits wrists under about 17cm best; the 40.5mm is the most popular all-rounder.

Does the Certina DS Action hold its value? Like most watches under $1,000, it typically loses 20-40% at resale. Certina’s credibility in informed watch communities means demand is steadier than fashion-adjacent brands, but buy it to wear and enjoy, not as an investment.
