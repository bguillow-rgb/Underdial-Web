Glycine occupies an odd corner of the Swiss watch market: genuine heritage, real Swiss movements, prices comfortably under $500, and an ownership story that makes enthusiasts nervous. If you've typed the brand name into a search bar and landed here, you've probably already noticed that opinions range from "hidden gem" to "avoid at all costs." Neither camp is entirely right. Here's what actually matters for a buyer spending under $1,000.

## What even is Glycine, and why haven't I heard of it?

*A question we hear often:*

Glycine was founded in Biel, Switzerland in 1914, the same watchmaking valley that produced Tissot, Omega, and Rolex. Its most significant contribution to the industry was the Airman, launched in 1953: a 24-hour automatic pilot's watch designed to display multiple time zones simultaneously. That aviation identity defined the brand for decades.

The reason you may not have heard of it is partly structural. Compared to other brands in its price sector, Glycine doesn't market itself anywhere near as aggressively, and since it holds no particular advantage in awareness or exposure, it has historically compensated with lower price margins. Serious watch buyers sometimes stumble across it and wonder why it's so cheap for what it appears to offer. The short answer: it has never invested heavily in advertising, and after a 2016 acquisition, it no longer carries independent prestige as a talking point.

## Wait -- Invicta owns Glycine now? Should I be worried?

This is the question that dominates every Glycine forum thread, and it deserves a direct answer.

Yes, Invicta acquired Glycine in 2016, and that created genuine concern in the watch community. Some former fans said the brand lost its identity. That reaction is understandable: Invicta's own reputation is built on heavily discounted, inflated-MSRP watches that bear little resemblance to what Glycine historically represented.

The practical reality is more nuanced. The quality of Glycine watches is much higher than a similarly priced Invicta -- you get quite a bit of watch for around $320. The Swiss Made designation has been maintained. The movement supply chain, primarily ETA and Sellita ebauches, hasn't been downgraded. What has changed is intangible: the perception of brand independence is gone, and that hurts resale value more than it hurts daily wearability.

If you're buying Glycine to wear and enjoy, the Invicta parentage matters less than it looks on paper. If you're buying with any thought of selling later, it matters a lot, and you should weight that accordingly.

## What do you actually get for $250-$500 with Glycine?

The cheapest Glycine models start around $250 and the most expensive barely reach $500, placing the brand squarely in the low-to-mid budget tier. That bracket is intensely competitive -- it's where Seiko, Orient, and the entry end of Tissot all compete. So what does Glycine bring?

| Feature | Glycine Combat 6 (40mm) | Orient Kamasu | Tissot Gentleman (quartz) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Movement | ETA 2824 / Sellita SW200 | Orient F6922 in-house | ETA 251.262 quartz |
| Crystal | Sapphire | Sapphire | Sapphire |
| Water resistance | 100m | 200m | 100m |
| Case origin | Swiss Made | Japanese | Swiss Made |
| Street price | ~$280-$350 | ~$300-$380 | ~$250-$300 |
| Resale value | Low | Low-Moderate | Moderate |

The genuine advantage of a Glycine automatic at this price is the movement. Some argued after the Invicta acquisition that Glycines lost their independent value, but they still carry ETA 2824-2 movements: reliable Swiss ebauches found in watches costing considerably more. Getting a proven Swiss automatic caliber with sapphire crystal under $350 street price is a real value proposition, even if the brand story is complicated. ETA-based Glycine automatics tend to perform within roughly plus or minus 4 seconds daily, which is a solid result for the price.

## Which Glycine models are actually worth considering?

*Readers frequently ask:*

There are two lines worth your attention in the under-$600 bracket.

**The Combat 6** is Glycine's everyday tool watch: a clean, legible automatic with a round case, simple dial layout, and the ETA or Sellita movement mentioned above. The 36mm version suits smaller wrists well and is unusually compact for a Swiss automatic. Most owners rate it highly for comfort and treat it as a go-anywhere daily wearer.

**The Airman** is the more interesting choice for anyone drawn to the pilot-watch archetype. Its 24-hour dial tracks two time zones simultaneously and reads differently from every other watch at this price. The design is genuinely polarizing -- the 24-hour layout takes adjustment -- but if the concept appeals to you, nothing else in the sub-$600 segment does it quite like this. If you're already exploring that archetype, our [pilot watch vs field watch guide](/pilot-watch-vs-field-watch) explains the broader category differences worth knowing before you commit.

One caveat on both lines: Glycine's MSRP is almost never the real price. Budget-conscious buyers should check authorized retailers and reputable grey-market sources before comparing against competitors -- the actual purchase price is typically 30-45% below list.

## How does Glycine stack up against Tissot and Hamilton at similar prices?

*This one comes up a lot:*

This is the honest version of the comparison most buyers actually face. At street prices, a Glycine Combat 6 and an entry-level Tissot Gentleman often land within $50-$80 of each other. Here's how to think about the trade-off.

**Choose Tissot if:** brand recognition matters to you, you plan to sell eventually, or you want solid after-sales support from a Swatch Group dealer network. Tissot offers better brand recognition and resale than Glycine, even at similar price points.

**Choose Glycine if:** you want a Swiss automatic movement rather than a quartz Tissot at the same price, you're drawn to the Airman's aviation aesthetic (there's no real Tissot equivalent), or you've found a compelling sale price.

Hamilton enters the conversation above $450 and brings a stronger brand identity: military heritage, Swatch Group backing, genuine collector interest, with similar Swiss automatic movements. If your budget can reach $500-$550 street price, [Hamilton vs Seiko](/hamilton-vs-seiko) is probably the more productive comparison to work through.

## Does Glycine hold its value at all?

No -- and you should go in with that expectation set clearly. Resale value is Glycine's weakest point. The Invicta parentage has suppressed secondary-market demand, and the brand simply doesn't carry the enthusiast cachet that keeps Seiko Presage or Tissot PRX prices reasonably stable on platforms like Chrono24. On the broader pre-owned watch market, roughly two thirds of watches sold in 2026 closed below 250 euros, and only 8.1% reached 1,000 euros or more -- meaning affordable watches as a category already face a soft resale floor, and Glycine sits at the softer end of that floor.

If resale trajectory is part of your decision, this is a real strike against the brand. Buy it because you want to wear it, not as any kind of store of value.

## So is Glycine actually worth buying?

Glycine is a reasonable low-to-mid budget alternative to pricier brands in the aviation and tool watch space. In the $300-$500 bracket, it qualifies as a solid entry-level Swiss option.

The honest verdict: Glycine is worth buying if you've found it at a discounted street price, you want a Swiss automatic movement under $400, and you're genuinely drawn to its design -- either the clean Combat 6 or the distinctive Airman. It is not worth buying if brand recognition matters to you, if you're thinking about resale, or if you want a watch with strong enthusiast community support behind it. ## Frequently asked questions

**Is Glycine still Swiss-made after being acquired by Invicta?**
Yes. Glycine watches are still assembled in Switzerland and carry the Swiss Made designation. Invicta's ownership is a corporate arrangement; the production pipeline and Swiss certification have been maintained across the core Combat and Airman lines.

**How does Glycine compare to Tissot at the same price?**
Tissot generally wins on brand recognition, resale value, and after-sales support. Glycine competes on distinct design -- especially the multi-timezone Airman dial -- and occasionally on outright sale price. If resale matters, choose Tissot. If you want something more unusual at a real-money discount, Glycine is worth considering.

**What movement does Glycine use in its watches?**
Most Glycine automatics run on ETA or Sellita movements -- specifically the ETA 2824-2 or close equivalents -- which are reliable, widely serviced Swiss ebauches. This is a genuine positive: getting a proven Swiss movement at Glycine's price point is good value.

**Are Glycine watches sold at a heavy discount from MSRP?**
Almost always. Glycine's MSRP is rarely the actual street price -- authorized retailers and grey-market discounters regularly sell them 30-50% below list. Factor that in when evaluating value: the real price is typically much lower than what the brand's website shows.

**Which Glycine model is the best starting point for a first-time buyer?**
The Combat 6 Automatic in 36mm or 40mm is the most straightforward entry point: it uses a proven Swiss movement, has a clean legible dial, sapphire crystal, and street prices around $250-$320. The Airman is more interesting but its busy multi-timezone dial takes some getting used to.
